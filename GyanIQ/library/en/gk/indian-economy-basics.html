Indian Economy Basics: GDP, RBI, Taxes and Budget

GK & Current Affairs · 3 min read · Updated: October 2026

Economy questions appear in almost every SSC, Railway, Banking and UPSC Prelims paper, and most of them test basic ideas rather than calculations. This note covers the building blocks: type of economy, sectors, GDP, the RBI, taxes, the budget and NITI Aayog, in short points you can revise quickly before the exam.

Mixed economy and three sectors

India follows a mixed economy, where the private sector and the government (public sector) work side by side. Economic activity is grouped into three sectors.

SectorActivitiesExamples
PrimaryUsing natural resources directlyFarming, fishing, mining
SecondaryMaking goods from raw materialManufacturing, construction
TertiaryProviding servicesBanking, transport, education, trade

GDP and GNP

  • GDP (Gross Domestic Product): total value of final goods and services produced inside the country in one year.
  • GNP (Gross National Product): GDP plus net income earned from abroad by the country's residents.
  • Only final goods are counted, so the same item is not counted twice.

RBI, currency, repo rate and inflation

  • The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) was established in 1935 and its headquarters is in Mumbai.
  • RBI is the central bank: it regulates banks and issues currency notes, except the one-rupee note.
  • Coins and the one-rupee note are issued by the Finance Ministry (Government of India).
  • Repo rate: the rate at which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks.
  • Inflation: a continuous rise in the general price level, which reduces what money can buy.

Direct and indirect taxes

A direct tax is paid by the person on whom it is levied. An indirect tax can be passed on to the consumer. GST, introduced in 2017, is an indirect tax.

PointDirect taxIndirect tax
BurdenStays with the payerShifted to the consumer
ExamplesIncome tax, corporate taxGST, customs duty

Budget basics and NITI Aayog

  • Revenue budget: day-to-day receipts such as taxes and day-to-day spending such as salaries.
  • Capital budget: assets and liabilities, such as loans, and spending on infrastructure.
  • Fiscal deficit: total government expenditure minus total receipts excluding borrowings.
  • NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015 and works as the government's policy think tank.

Practice Quiz

Q1. The Reserve Bank of India was established in the year:

Q2. The one-rupee note is issued by:

Correct answer: D) Ministry of Finance. The one-rupee note and all coins are issued by the Finance Ministry; RBI issues other notes.

Q3. GST, introduced in India in 2017, is a:
Show answer

Correct answer: B) Indirect tax. GST is charged on goods and services and can be passed on to the consumer, so it is an indirect tax.

Q4. NITI Aayog replaced which body in 2015?
Show answer

Correct answer: A) Planning Commission. NITI Aayog replaced the Planning Commission in 2015.

Q5. Banking and transport belong to which sector?
Show answer

Correct answer: B) Tertiary. Banking and transport are services, so they come under the tertiary sector.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between GDP and GNP?

GDP counts output produced inside the country's borders. GNP is GDP plus net income earned from abroad by the country's residents.

Who prints the one-rupee note in India?

The one-rupee note is issued by the Finance Ministry of the Government of India, not by the RBI. RBI issues all other currency notes.
