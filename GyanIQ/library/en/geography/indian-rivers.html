Indian Rivers: Himalayan and Peninsular Systems Explained

Geography · 3 min read · Updated: October 2026

India's rivers are divided into two groups, Himalayan and Peninsular, and this division explains almost every fact asked in geography exams. Questions on origins, tributaries, the longest river and rivers that flow west are very common in SSC, Railway, Police and UPSC prelims. Use this note to learn the systems in a clear order.

Himalayan vs Peninsular rivers

The difference in source and water supply is the key idea.

FeatureHimalayanPeninsular
Water sourceSnow melt and rainMainly rain
FlowPerennial, all yearSeasonal, dry in summer
CourseLong, with gorges and big deltasShorter, on older plateau
ExamplesGanga, Indus, BrahmaputraGodavari, Krishna, Kaveri

Himalayan systems

  • Ganga: starts as the Bhagirathi from Gangotri glacier and meets the Alaknanda at Devprayag. Yamuna, Ghaghra, Gandak and Kosi join from the north, Son from the south. It ends in the Bay of Bengal.
  • Yamuna: rises at Yamunotri and joins the Ganga at Prayagraj.
  • Brahmaputra: rises in Tibet, enters India in Arunachal Pradesh, flows through Assam and reaches Bangladesh.
  • Indus: rises near Mansarovar in Tibet, flows through Ladakh and has the tributaries Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej. It falls into the Arabian Sea.

Peninsular rivers flowing east

  • Godavari: the longest Peninsular river, called Dakshin Ganga, rises near Nashik.
  • Krishna: rises near Mahabaleshwar in the Western Ghats.
  • Kaveri: rises in Karnataka and flows through Tamil Nadu.
  • Mahanadi: rises in Chhattisgarh and flows through Odisha. All four fall into the Bay of Bengal.

West-flowing rivers

Narmada and Tapi are the two big Peninsular rivers that flow west into the Arabian Sea. They run through rift valleys (long, sunken blocks of land), so they do not form deltas but estuaries. Narmada rises at Amarkantak, and Tapi rises in the Satpura range.

Practice Quiz

Q1. Which is the longest river of Peninsular India?
Show answer

Correct answer: B) Godavari. Godavari is the longest Peninsular river and is called Dakshin Ganga.

Q2. Narmada flows into which water body?
Show answer

Correct answer: C) Arabian Sea. Narmada flows west through a rift valley and ends in the Arabian Sea.

Q3. At which place does Bhagirathi meet Alaknanda to form the Ganga?
Show answer

Correct answer: D) Devprayag. The two streams meet at Devprayag and the combined river is called the Ganga.

Q4. Which of these is a Himalayan river?
Show answer

Correct answer: B) Brahmaputra. Brahmaputra rises in Tibet and is a snow and rain fed Himalayan river.

Q5. Delhi is situated on the bank of which river?
Show answer

Correct answer: A) Yamuna. Delhi lies on the right bank of the Yamuna.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Narmada and Tapi flow west?

They flow through rift valleys that slope towards the Arabian Sea, unlike the other Peninsular rivers that follow the eastward slope of the plateau.

What is the difference between Himalayan and Peninsular rivers?

Himalayan rivers are perennial because snow melt feeds them, while Peninsular rivers depend mostly on rain and can run low in summer.
