Number Series in Reasoning: Types, Method and Solved Examples

Reasoning & Puzzles · 3 min read · Updated: October 2026

A number series is a list of numbers that follows a hidden rule. You must find the rule and give the missing or next number. Questions on number series appear in almost every reasoning section of SSC, Railway, Banking and Police exams. Once you know the common patterns, most of these questions can be solved in under 30 seconds.

Common types of series

TypeRuleExample
ArithmeticAdd or subtract the same number5, 9, 13, 17 (+4)
GeometricMultiply or divide by the same number3, 6, 12, 24 (x2)
Squares and cubesSquares or cubes of consecutive numbers1, 4, 9, 16 or 1, 8, 27, 64
AlternatingTwo series mixed in odd and even places2, 10, 4, 20, 6, 30
Difference of differencesThe gaps themselves form a pattern2, 3, 5, 8, 12 (gaps 1, 2, 3, 4)
Prime numbersPrimes in order2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13
Fibonacci-likeEach term is the sum of the previous two1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8

Step-by-step method

  • Find the difference between neighbouring terms. If it is constant, the series is arithmetic.
  • If the difference changes, check the ratio. A constant ratio means a geometric series.
  • If both fail, look at the gaps of the gaps, or compare the terms with squares and cubes (try n squared plus or minus 1).
  • If the series looks jumbled, split it into odd-place and even-place terms and treat them as two separate series.

Solved examples

  • 3, 5, 9, 15, 23, ? The gaps are 2, 4, 6, 8, so the next gap is 10 and the answer is 33.
  • 2, 6, 18, 54, ? Each term is multiplied by 3, so the answer is 162.
  • 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, ? These are squares minus 1 (4-1, 9-1, 16-1 and so on), so the next is 49 - 1 = 48.
  • 10, 5, 20, 10, 30, 15, ? Odd places 10, 20, 30 and even places 5, 10, 15 both rise steadily, so the answer is 40.

Odd-one-out series

Here you must find the number that does not follow the rule that the others share. Check for primes, squares, cubes, multiples of a number and odd or even properties. In 2, 3, 5, 9, 11 every number except 9 is prime, so 9 is the odd one. In 4, 9, 16, 24, 25 all are squares except 24. Always test two or three rules before you decide.

Exam Tip: Memorise squares up to 30, cubes up to 10 and primes up to 50 to spot patterns fast. For practice, try the logic games Mind-Matrix and PuzzleMaster on GyanIQ.

Practice Quiz

Q1. Find the next number: 4, 7, 10, 13, ?
Show answer

Correct answer: B) 16. The series increases by 3 each time, so 13 + 3 = 16.

Q2. Find the next number: 2, 4, 8, 16, ?
Show answer

Correct answer: C) 32. Each term is double the previous one, so 16 x 2 = 32.

Q3. Find the next number: 1, 2, 4, 7, 11, ?
Show answer

Correct answer: D) 16. The gaps are 1, 2, 3, 4, so the next gap is 5 and the answer is 11 + 5 = 16.

Q4. Find the next number: 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, ?
Show answer

Correct answer: A) 48. The terms are squares minus 1: 4-1, 9-1, 16-1, 25-1, 36-1. The next is 49 - 1 = 48.

Q5. Find the odd one out: 8, 27, 64, 100, 125
Show answer

Correct answer: C) 100. 8, 27, 64 and 125 are cubes of 2, 3, 4 and 5. 100 is not a cube.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I solve number series questions quickly?

First check the difference between terms, then the ratio, then squares, cubes and alternating patterns. Knowing squares, cubes and primes by heart saves the most time.

What if no pattern is visible in a series?

Split the series into odd-place and even-place terms, or check whether the gaps form a pattern. Many hard series are two simple series mixed together.
